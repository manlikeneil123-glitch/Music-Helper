Accessibility

We want everyone to be able to practise with Stave.

What we aim for

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA.

What works today

  • Everything can be used with a keyboard, with a visible focus ring.
  • Buttons and controls have names that screen readers announce, including icon-only ones.
  • You can zoom up to 200% and more; text wraps rather than being cut off.
  • Text contrast meets 4.5:1 in both Day and Night themes, checked by an automated test on every release.
  • Moving effects stop if your device asks for reduced motion.
  • Note results use shapes as well as colour: missed notes are dashed, unclear ones are hollow, and every result is also written out in words.
  • You can play with a MIDI keyboard instead of the microphone.

Settings that make it easier

  • Theme: Day (light) or Night (dark).
  • Still scenery: turns off the moving background and entrance animations.
  • Show me around: a step-by-step tour that highlights each part of the screen. Use the arrow keys or Next and Back; Esc ends it.
  • Vibration: on or off.

All are in Settings (tap your avatar, then Settings).

Known limits

  • Sheet music is drawn as pictures. Screen readers can't read the notation itself, though the results of a check are given in words.
  • Microphone checking needs the app to hear the piano clearly; it can't be used in a noisy room.
  • Timed parts (count-ins, the 30-second sight-reading look) can't be paused yet.
  • Swiping flashcards needs a touch screen or mouse; with a keyboard, use the left and right arrow keys or the Missed it and Got it buttons.
  • We haven't yet tested with every screen reader. VoiceOver and TalkBack testing is still to do.

Report a problem

If something is hard to use, tell us on our GitHub page and we'll fix it or find another way.
